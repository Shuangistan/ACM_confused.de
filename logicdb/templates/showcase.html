Approve once.

Take the decision logic out of the model and put it in an inspectable database. Then put human oversight on the logic rather than on individual decisions.

The problem with a human in the loop

Per-decision oversight does not scale, and it degrades into rubber-stamping exactly where it matters most — when the reviewer cannot independently verify the output they are signing. A signature on an unverifiable output transfers blame without improving the decision.

The failure is not of oversight. It is a failure of unit. Review a rule once, and it governs every future case whose facts match it.

36.8 decisions governed per act of review, over 1,000 real credit applications — sixteen reviews, 589 decisions, and the ratio still climbing as the rule base matures.

What makes it hold

Determinism

Inference is a terminating Datalog fixpoint. Same facts, same rules, same answer, every time.

Explainability

An answer arrives with the proof that produced it — not a narrative written afterwards to match it.

Oversight that binds

A critical decision is structurally incapable of resting on a rule no human approved.

The commitment underneath all three: the agent proposes; only the engine infers. A language model drafts candidate facts and rules; it never produces a decision. Nondeterminism is quarantined upstream of a human approval gate, and everything downstream is reproducible.

rule r0003 [p=0.80, origin=mined, status=approved, by="reviewer-jd",
            support=214, used=5, right="3/4"]
  high_risk(A) <- overdrawn(A), thin_savings(A).

used is the amortisation count. right is a live tally, so a rule's strength is auditable rather than asserted. Identity is a canonical hash — variables renumbered, body sorted — so the same logic re-proposed in different syntax is recognised as already settled.

See it refuse to guess

Answer: P = [0.00, 0.70]
  The interval is 0.70 wide because facts are missing. It is not a
  confidence interval over a model — it spans every way the unknowns
  could resolve.

  [UNKNOWN] not app_gap has mitigating circumstances
      (ASSUMED, not established)

Missing information, ranked by whether it would change anything:
  app_gap has a guarantor   [DECISIVE]
      if true -> 0.00, if false -> 0.70

A missing fact produces an interval, not a number. The system names the one question that would settle it, stays silent about unknowns that would change nothing, and distinguishes assumed from established so an interval endpoint cannot be misread as a finding.

Against our own earlier prototype

Before building the rule base we built the conventional version: a hundred simulated screening agents over an 8,000-applicant, 1,000-hire recruitment campaign. It is a useful baseline precisely because we wrote it — and running it exposes two things its interface does not say.

It flags 5,621 of 8,000 CVs for review, 4,012 of them cases where the requirements were met. And its interface says of agent rejections that "each can be reopened", yet 90% are never shown to anyone.

Does the miner find the right rules?

The five near-misses are the interesting part. Two are wrong in ways a reviewer can check: one grants eligibility on a recent award, which policy says should trigger referral; another reproduces a route with the capital-limit guard silently dropped. Both sit at 83–85% precision and look entirely respectable. That is the case for a human reading rules.

And it found a hole in our own written policy

We authored a lending policy, extracted it into eight cited rules, and the system then declined to decide 11% of cases. One clause covers elevated risk without security, another covers security without elevated risk — neither covers an applicant with both. We had not noticed. A scoring model would have produced a confident number for every one of them.

Design principles

Each was earned by a defect we shipped and then fixed, not derived from first principles.

  1. Put oversight where it amortises

    If your oversight cost per decision is flat, you have chosen the wrong unit.

  2. Absence is not denial

    "No applicable logic" and "no" are different answers and must read differently. Competing outcomes are never normalised, so ignorance stays visible instead of becoming a confident 50/50.

  3. Unknowns must survive negation

    A fact blocked by a missing input is itself unknown. We shipped this wrong twice — an applicant was declined on an assumption no one had made.

  4. Approve the logic, not the text

    Identity must be invariant under renaming and reordering, or approve-once means nothing.

  5. Evidence follows provenance

    A document rule asks does this encode the clause? A mined rule asks is this correlation also a reason? Render them identically and the reviewer answers the easier question twice.

  6. Make guarantees structural, not procedural

    Our policies forbid decisions resting on protected attributes. That clause is deliberately not a rule — a rule can state a condition, but not the absence of a hidden correlation. The attribute is simply absent from the vocabulary, so nothing can be written or mined over it.

  7. State the assumption in the output

    Combining supports by noisy-OR treats them as independent evidence, which is frequently wrong. It appears in every explanation rather than hiding inside the arithmetic.

  8. A system that cannot be wrong cannot be audited

    Logic whose rejections are correct by construction offers contestability and can never exercise it. Build the ground truth that lets a decision be wrong, or the review queue is decoration.

  9. The price of speed must be enumerable

    Provisional rules keep routine work moving; rejecting one names every decision it touched, across sessions.

What we have not shown

No human has reviewed a rule in this system. The amortisation figure is arithmetic over a working prototype, not evidence that reviewers review rules well. If they cannot, the argument fails — and nothing here would currently tell us.

That experiment is unusually clean, because the synthetic pack supplies ground truth for rules: show reviewers the eight proposals above and measure whether they approve the correct three.

Credit assignment is crude. Every rule in a derivation is marked right or wrong together, so a determination rule is blamed for failures caused by its premises.

Rejection flags but does not reverse. The system names the decisions a withdrawn rule touched and stops. Whether that invalidates them is a judgement it should not make alone.

The head-to-head is not like-for-like. The baseline is our own earlier prototype, and the two partition work differently.

Accuracy figures are not benchmarks. They score invented policies against real data. The system detecting that its own policy is poorly fitted is the machinery working, not a capability claim.

Datalog inference, three-valued evidence, and a persisted append-only governance record. Credit data: UCI Statlog German Credit (Hofmann, 1994). Benefits and recruitment domains are synthetic.
