\begin{frame}{The remedy everyone reaches for}
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{center}
    {\Large A person reviews each consequential decision\\before it takes
     effect.}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1.6em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}\color{muted}
    This is called meaningful human control.
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{It fails in two directions at once}
  \vspace{1.2em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{1.1em}
    \item \textbf{At volume it is unaffordable.} So it is skipped, or performed
      at a rate that makes it ceremonial.
    \item \textbf{Shown a recommendation without its reasoning, a reviewer can
      only agree.} The check meant to catch errors becomes a signature that
      launders them.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{1.4em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}\color{muted}
    Tuning the threshold manages the cost. It does not touch the cause.
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

% ===================================================================
\begin{frame}{Two questions, one mechanism}
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{.47\textwidth}
      \textbf{\color{petrol}How much oversight?}
      \vspace{.5em}
      \begin{itemize}
        \setlength\itemsep{.45em}
        \item harm if wrong
        \item reversibility
        \item is a right engaged
        \item how complete the evidence is
      \end{itemize}
      \vspace{.6em}
      {\small\color{muted} A \emph{policy} over consequences.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{.47\textwidth}
      \textbf{\color{amber}What is the decision?}
      \vspace{.5em}
      \begin{itemize}
        \setlength\itemsep{.45em}
        \item conditions
        \item conclusions
        \item a derivation
      \end{itemize}
      \vspace{.6em}
      {\small\color{muted} \emph{Logic.}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{1.3em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}
    Answering both with one mechanism forces the trade-off.\\
    \textbf{Answering them separately dissolves it.}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Half one: the ladder}
  \vspace{.4em}
\begin{lstlisting}
if (harm >= 9 and rights) or (harm >= 8 and reversibility <= 3):
    human_only          # AI may not decide
if harm >= 6 or rights or confidence < 70:
    human_approval      # a person approves first
if harm <= 3 and reversibility >= 7:
    ai_with_oversight   # bounded, logged, reversible
ai_recommends           # propose; do not act
\end{lstlisting}
  \vspace{.7em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.4em}
    \item Nine lines, in a module that \textbf{imports nothing at all}.
    \item A module that cannot reach a network cannot be talked into anything.
    \item It never sees the answer.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The ladder exists twice — so we test that}
  \vspace{1em}
  It runs in Python on the server, and in JavaScript in the page so the
  sliders respond without a round trip.
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.6em}
    \item Two copies of the rule allocating human oversight can drift with
      \textbf{nothing failing}.
    \item The page would show one tier while the server acted on another.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{1.1em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}
    A test extracts \texttt{classify} \emph{from the shipped page}, runs it
    under Node,\\ and compares \textbf{all 1{,}000 input combinations}.
    \\[.5em]
    {\color{muted}\small Mutating one threshold: twenty disagreements,
    each one named.}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Half two: the logic database}
  \vspace{.6em}
\begin{lstlisting}
will_get_wet(C) <- going_out(C), raining(C),
                   not has_umbrella(C).
\end{lstlisting}
  \vspace{.9em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.55em}
    \item Umbrella recorded \textbf{absent} $\rightarrow$ the rule fires.
    \item Umbrella \textbf{unmentioned} $\rightarrow$ it does not, and the head
      is \emph{blocked}, not false.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{1.1em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}\color{muted}
    Facts come from a model reading free text. Under a closed world,\\
    silence becomes evidence against a person one round later.\\[.4em]
    {\color{brick}\textbf{We shipped that defect twice in the predecessor.}}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The gate asks one question}
  \vspace{.8em}
  \begin{center}\large
    \emph{Does an approved rule cover this case?}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Approved rule fires} & \textbf{Nothing fires} \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{human\_only}        & proceeds & \textbf{\color{brick}waits} \\
    \texttt{human\_approval}    & proceeds & \textbf{\color{brick}waits} \\
    \texttt{ai\_with\_oversight}& proceeds & proceeds, sampled \\
    \texttt{ai\_recommends}     & proceeds & proceeds, sampled \\

  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1.1em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}\color{muted}

\begin{frame}{Why approved logic proceeds even at the top tier}
  \vspace{1.2em}
  \begin{center}\large
    A rule an expert approved \textbf{is their decision.}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{center}
    They saw the clause. They accepted that any case matching its body\\
    gets its conclusion. Applying it executes a call they have made.
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1.3em}
  \pause
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.5em}
    \item Asking again for every matching case is how oversight becomes
      ceremony.
    \item Approval is of a \textbf{clause}, whose body states exactly what it
      claims.
    \item A proportion is audited anyway — 50\% for \texttt{human\_only} logic,
      5\% routine, \textbf{never zero}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What broke}
  \vspace{.7em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.62em}
    \item \textbf{A greeting was classified.} ``Hi'' $\rightarrow$ \emph{human
      approval required}. The estimator said ``no decision described'' and
      scored confidence 0; the ladder read \texttt{< 70} and demanded a person.
    \item \textbf{Extraction returned prose.} Folded into the estimator's call
      to save a request — one prompt, two jobs. Every fact dropped at parse
      time. Cheaper, and useless.
    \item \textbf{The proposer drafted the opposite rule.} For a disputed debt
      with no notice, it required \texttt{not debt\_disputed} and
      \texttt{formal\_notice\_given}.
    \item \textbf{Parked inquiries contributed nothing.} The most consequential
      decisions were the ones whose logic was never written down.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The one we got wrong four times}
  \vspace{1em}
  We called the intake gate flaky. Measured, it was \textbf{deterministically
  wrong}:
  \vspace{.9em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lr}
    \toprule
    ``Should we reorder \textbf{toner} now?''     & 6/6 hand over \\
    ``Should we reorder \textbf{envelopes} now?'' & 0/6 hand over \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{.8em}
  The only difference: \emph{toner} appeared in the prompt's example list.
  \vspace{1.1em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}
    Four rounds of rewording, inconsistent each time $\rightarrow$
    \textbf{change the shape, not the words.}\\[.5em]
    Ask the \emph{narrow} question — was this purely conversational? —
    and hand over unless yes.\\[.4em]
    {\color{muted}\small Recognising a greeting is reliable.
    Recognising a decision is not.}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Coverage: the only number that matters}
  \vspace{.8em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Run A} & \textbf{Run B} \\
    \midrule
    Decided from approved logic & 2 / 12 & 3 / 12 \\
    Parked for a person         & 5 / 12 & 4 / 12 \\
    \textbf{Coverage}           & \textbf{18\%} & \textbf{30\%} \\
    Approved rules              & 7 & 5 \\
    Distinct predicates coined  & 19 & 15 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1em}
  \pause
  \begin{center}\color{muted}
    Twelve cases, empty database, every proposed rule approved.\\
    We report the range, not the better number.
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Why it is not higher — in the system's own words}
  \vspace{.6em}
  The vocabulary the database built for itself:
\begin{lstlisting}
reorder_toner          reorder_paper
toner_stock_low        paper_stock_low
toner_stock_depleted   paper_stock_depleted
\end{lstlisting}
  \vspace{.7em}
  \begin{center}
    Six terms and two rules for one idea.
  \end{center}
  \vspace{.9em}
  \pause
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.45em}
    \item Toner logic cannot decide a paper case $\rightarrow$ it arrives
      uncovered $\rightarrow$ it coins its own near-duplicate.
    \item Where the entity \emph{stayed out} of the term ---
      \texttt{in\_arrears}, \texttt{debt\_disputed} --- three cases were
      decided by the first one's logic.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{.7em}
  \begin{center}\color{petrol}\textbf{
    \texttt{reorder(C, X) $\leftarrow$ stock\_low(C, X), not depleted(C, X).}
  }\\[.3em]
  {\color{muted}\small The engine is first-order and can already express this.
  Nothing proposes it.}\end{center}
\end{frame}

% ===================================================================
\begin{frame}{What we have not measured}
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{.75em}
    \item \textbf{No expert has used this.} Every approval in those runs was
      granted by a script.
    \item \textbf{One model.} All figures are Haiku 4.5. Whether a stronger
      model coins a reusable vocabulary is the bottleneck, untested and cheap
      to test.
    \item \textbf{Two runs is not a measurement.} 18 vs 30 per cent on
      identical input.
    \item \textbf{Top-tier conduct is a prompt, not a mechanism.} It held under
      direct pressure in every trial — three trials is an anecdote.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vspace{1.6em}
  \begin{center}
    {\Large Per-decision oversight degrades into ceremony.}\\[1em]
    {\Large Rule-level oversight amortises ---}\\[.4em]
    {\Large \textbf{but only if rules generalise, and ours do not yet.}}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1.8em}
  \begin{center}\color{muted}
    A fifth of cases, decided the same way every time,\\
    with a derivation attached and a named person behind the logic.\\[1em]
    The path from a fifth to a majority runs through one mechanism\\
    we can describe, have not built, and can see the shape of\\
    in our own system's vocabulary.
  \end{center}
